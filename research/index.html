RESEARCH · HUMAN–ENVIRONMENT RELATIONSHIPS

The future
we hope for.

Understanding how we shape our environment—and how a changing environment shapes our lives.

From understanding change
to informing adaptation.

Climate change is reshaping the conditions in which people and ecosystems depend on water. At the same time, urbanization, land-use change, and water management alter the movement and quality of water. These processes connect the atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere, and biosphere, with consequences for communities and ecosystems.

My research interests center on this two-way relationship. I want to investigate how human activities affect the hydrologic cycle and how environmental feedbacks can inform adaptation. My work so far approaches this broader goal through coastal resilience assessment, land-use and habitat modeling, and the spatial identification of wastewater facilities.

COASTAL ECOLOGICAL RESILIENCE · PUBLISHED RESEARCH

How resilient are our
coastal landscapes?

China’s coastal zone · Feb 2024 – Jul 2025
Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology
Supervisor: Chengyi Zhao

Coastal landscapes bring human development and ecological processes into close contact. This project asked how ecological resilience varies across China’s coastal zone, how it changes over time, and how its future may differ under alternative climate and development scenarios.

SELECTED FINDINGS · PUBLISHED STUDY

Ecological resilience (ER) declined overall during 1995–2020. For 2030, projected mean ER was highest under SSP1-2.6 and lowest under SSP5-8.5. ER is a dimensionless index; the annual change is in index units per year, not a percentage. These are findings of the coauthored study.

Compare the five projected futures for 2030

Reading the landscape

The study used the Pressure–State–Response (PSR) framework to assess ecological resilience at the pixel scale. It connected indicators of environmental pressure, ecosystem condition, and responses, then examined spatial and temporal patterns and future trajectories under SSP–RCP scenarios.

SSP–RCP scenarios combine socioeconomic pathways with climate forcing pathways.

My contribution

I contributed data analysis to the resilience assessment and scenario comparisons. The work connected spatial patterns with questions about how human and environmental pressures may shape coastal futures.

I am a coauthor of the resulting article, published in Land in 2024.

A CONNECTED UNDERGRADUATE PROJECT

Land-use change, habitat quality, and future choices.

In a separate project, I led work coupling PLUS and InVEST to examine coastal land-use patterns and habitat quality from 2020 to 2050 under SSP126, SSP245, and SSP585. I assessed drivers of habitat-quality change and the contribution of land-cover changes to degradation.

Together, these experiences shaped my interest in how spatial assessment and scenario analysis can inform land-use planning and adaptation.

ADEQ PROJECT · WATER & ENVIRONMENTAL HEALTH

Making wastewater
infrastructure more visible.

Arizona, United States · Jan 2026 – Present · Ongoing
Arizona State University
Supervisor: Ruijie Zeng

The relationship between people and water is also built into everyday infrastructure. My current ADEQ project asks how spatial analysis can help locate on-site wastewater treatment facilities, including potentially unpermitted facilities in rural Arizona.

Building a spatial picture

I use GIS to analyze potential on-site wastewater locations across Arizona. The current study combines county parcels and buildings, sewer and public-water service areas, community characteristics, and available wastewater records in a county-specific identification workflow.

Candidate identifiers are normalized and matched within counties. Permit-supported candidates are retained; other records with verified explicit vacant-land labels are screened out. The resulting inventory provides a basis for targeted record review and further investigation.

Connecting infrastructure and environment

This work extends my interest in human–environment relationships from coastal land use to the water systems serving communities. Better information about wastewater infrastructure can support environmental health assessment and help identify where closer attention may be useful.

The project is ongoing; my current contribution centers on spatial analysis and facility identification.
